## Design Context

### Users
Analytics engineering, BI engineering, and data-platform hiring managers evaluating James quickly on desktop or mobile. Their job is to understand his positioning, verify technical depth, and find credible evidence that he can connect reliable data models to business decisions.

### Brand Personality
Credible, exacting, and business-minded. The experience should feel like a senior data practitioner who understands both the warehouse and the meeting where the metric gets used.

### Aesthetic Direction
Quiet technical portfolio: cool off-white surfaces, dark green-tinted text, one muted forest accent, a single sans-serif family, conventional reading widths, and real project diagrams presented as working artifacts. Avoid creative-agency styling, oversized editorial headlines, italic accent words, ornamental numbering, grain, neon-on-dark developer tropes, terminal simulations, generic card grids, decorative charts, and excessive technology pills.

### Design Principles
1. Lead with proof: concrete systems, model counts, tests, outcomes, and architecture before tool lists.
2. Make the career transition coherent: analytics engineering is an extension of ten years in BI, not a reset.
3. Separate evidence from aspiration: never claim production practices or outcomes that the linked work cannot verify.
4. Use visual structure to explain data flow, ownership, and decisions—not merely to decorate.
5. Keep every primary action and core piece of evidence accessible on mobile and with reduced motion.
